The job

Suddenly there are forty things to do, and no one hands you a system.

Certificates, banks, retirement accounts, utilities, insurers. Phone calls that need to be made twice, letters that need the right wording, receipts that need to be kept. Most executors run the whole thing from a notepad and a worry list. The Companion gives the job a home.

The story gets told again and again

Every organisation asks the same questions, and each one starts from the beginning. Phone calls get made twice because nobody wrote down the first one.

In the Companion: Contacts and Correspondence. Who you spoke to, when, what was said and what happens next.

Letters have to be worded from scratch

Each notification needs the right wording, and there is no easy way to remember which organisation has already been told.

In the Companion: ready-to-adapt letter templates and a notification register, so each organisation gets told once, properly.

The receipts pile up somewhere

Estate expenses come out of somebody's pocket first, and the record of that gets kept in a wallet, a text message or a memory.

In the Companion: The Money Log. Estate expenses, money in, and reimbursements, recorded as you go.

Someone has just died. Is this going to help me today, or is it more reading?

It is a working file, not a book to get through. Open The First Days, which is a steady checklist for the first week or two, and fill in that page. The rest can wait until you are standing in it. Nothing here needs to be read cover to cover for the next part to make sense.

Can I fill it in on paper instead of on a computer?

Either way. It is a fillable PDF you can type into, or print and fill in by hand. No account, no app, no subscription.

Do I need to be the named executor to use this?

No. It is written for executors and next of kin, and it is often the person quietly doing the running around who fills it in. Nothing in it depends on your title. If more than one of you is helping, one shared file is easier than three sets of notes.

Yes, and that is how many executors use it. There is a section for working with a lawyer, with question prep and meeting notes, so paid time gets spent on answers rather than catch-up. The Companion sits alongside professional advice, not in place of it.
